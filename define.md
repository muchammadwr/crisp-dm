The CRISP-DM (Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining) life cycle process model consists of six non-linear, iterative phases:

  * **Business Understanding**: Focuses on understanding the project's objectives and requirements from a business perspective. This knowledge is used to define the business problem, establish business and technical success criteria, and create an initial project plan.
  * **Data Understanding**: Begins with initial data collection and proceeds with activities to get familiar with the data. Key tasks include describing and exploring the data, identifying data quality issues, and uncovering initial insights or interesting subsets.
  * **Data Preparation**: Covers all activities required to construct the final dataset used for modeling from raw data. Common tasks include selecting relevant rows and attributes, cleaning data to fix or remove errors, deriving new variables, and merging or formatting datasets.
  * **Modeling**: Involves selecting and applying various modeling techniques (such as classification, clustering, or association algorithms) and fine-tuning their parameters to optimize performance. Because models may require specific data formats, this phase often loops back to data preparation.
  * **Evaluation**: Formally assesses the constructed models and findings against the defined business objectives to ensure the results meet organizational goals. The team reviews the process executed and decides whether to deploy the model or refine it through further iterations.
  * **Deployment**: Translates the insights and predictive models into actionable tools or processes within the business operations. Activities range from generating executive reports and strategy presentations to integrating models directly into enterprise software and data warehouses.
